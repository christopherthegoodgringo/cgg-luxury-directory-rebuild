Latin America’s higher-rated hotels, restaurants and bars

Discover Latin America’s finest hotels, restaurants and bars.

Explore a focused collection of hotels, restaurants and bars across Latin America. Compare destinations, discover standout stays and dining spots.

Argentina

Buenos Aires blends European architecture with a vibrant arts and nightlife scene. Patagonia offers some of the most dramatic landscapes in the southern hemisphere. Mendoza's wine country and the Andean foothills round out a destination with remarkable range.

Bolivia

Bolivia's Uyuni salt flat is one of the most otherworldly landscapes on Earth. The Andean altiplano reaches elevations that challenge and reward in equal measure. Lake Titicaca, on the border with Peru, holds centuries of Indigenous history and island communities.

Brazil

Rio de Janeiro's beaches, the Amazon rainforest and Salvador's Afro-Brazilian culture. The Amazon basin covers more than half the country and remains the largest tropical rainforest on the planet. Salvador brings centuries of Afro-Brazilian culture to life through music, cuisine and architecture.

Chile

Chile stretches over 4,000 kilometers along South America's Pacific edge, creating an extraordinary range of landscapes. The Atacama is the driest desert on Earth; Chilean Patagonia is a wilderness of glaciers, fjords and national parks. Santiago is a modern, walkable capital and Valparaíso a colorful port city with a distinct creative energy.

Colombia

Cartagena’s colonial walls, Coffee Region landscapes and the Caribbean coast. The Coffee Region offers lush green landscapes, traditional fincas and some of the finest coffee grown anywhere in the world. Beyond these, Colombia spans Pacific coast rainforest, Andean cities and the grasslands of the Llanos.

Costa Rica

Costa Rica packs an astonishing range of ecosystems into a small country — cloud forests, active volcanoes, Pacific and Caribbean beaches and dense jungle. Its national park system protects close to a quarter of the country's land and is one of the most visited in the world. San José serves as a base for exploring in every direction, with short drives reaching dramatically different terrain.

Dominican Republic

The Dominican Republic anchors the eastern half of Hispaniola and offers a compelling mix of beach, history and nature. Santo Domingo's Zona Colonial is the oldest continuously inhabited European city in the Americas and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Samaná Peninsula draws visitors for humpback whale season each winter and some of the country's most secluded beaches.

Ecuador

Ecuador's Galápagos Islands remain one of the world's great wildlife destinations, where evolution played out in near isolation for millions of years. The Andean highlands hold a chain of towering volcanoes including Cotopaxi and Chimborazo, with Quito — a UNESCO World Heritage city — sitting at 2,850 meters. The Amazon basin begins just east of the Andes, reached in hours from the capital.

El Salvador

El Salvador is Central America's smallest country and one of its most underrated, with a coastline that has become a reference point for surfing in the region. A chain of volcanoes runs through the country's interior, offering hiking through cloud forest and crater lakes. San Salvador blends a colonial past with a modern food and arts scene that continues to grow.

Guatemala

Guatemala is home to some of the most significant Maya archaeological sites in Mesoamerica, including Tikal, whose temples rise above the jungle canopy. Lake Atitlán, ringed by volcanoes and Indigenous villages, is considered one of the most beautiful lakes in the world. Antigua, a UNESCO-listed colonial city, sits in a valley between three volcanoes and serves as a natural base for the country.

Honduras

Honduras's Bay Islands — Roatán, Utila and Guanaja — sit on the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second largest in the world, and offer some of the most accessible scuba diving in the Caribbean. Copán, on the western border with Guatemala, is one of the most elaborately carved Maya sites ever discovered. The Caribbean coast stretches east toward the Mosquito Coast, a largely roadless expanse of jungle and lagoon.

Mexico

Mexico is one of the most visited countries in the world and one of the most varied — stretching from Baja California in the north to the Yucatán Peninsula in the south. Ancient sites including Chichen Itzá, Teotihuacán and Palenque sit alongside colonial cities like Oaxaca, San Cristóbal and Mérida. The Caribbean coast anchored by Tulum and Cancún and the Pacific beaches of Puerto Vallarta and Sayulita add a third dimension.

Nicaragua

Nicaragua offers a quieter, less-traveled version of Central America with a strong colonial heritage and striking natural scenery. Granada, on the shores of Lake Nicaragua, is one of the oldest Spanish colonial cities in the Americas and retains much of its original character. The Pacific coast draws surfers to towns like San Juan del Sur, while the Corn Islands offer a more remote Caribbean escape.

Panama

Panama City is a striking contrast of gleaming skyscrapers and a UNESCO-listed colonial quarter, Casco Viejo, within a short walk of each other. The Panama Canal remains one of the great engineering achievements of the modern era and is a compelling visit even for non-engineers. Beyond the capital, the San Blas archipelago offers island-hopping with the Guna people, and Bocas del Toro draws travelers with Caribbean reef and jungle.

Paraguay

Paraguay is one of South America's least-visited countries and one of its most genuinely off-the-beaten-track destinations. The Jesuit missions of the Trinidad and Jesús ruins — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — represent an extraordinary chapter in colonial-era history. Asunción, one of the oldest capitals in South America, sits on the Paraguay River with a compact historic core that rewards slow exploration.

Peru

Machu Picchu, Lima’s celebrated food scene and the Sacred Valley of the Incas. Lima has become one of Latin America’s leading culinary capitals, with a restaurant scene that draws food travelers from around the world. The Sacred Valley, Cusco’s colonial center and the floating islands of Lake Titicaca round out a country with extraordinary depth.

Uruguay

Colonia del Sacramento's cobbled streets, Punta del Este beaches and Montevideo. Colonia del Sacramento, a short ferry from Buenos Aires, preserves a Portuguese colonial quarter that is unlike anywhere else in the region. Punta del Este draws a South American summer crowd each January with its beaches, art installations and restaurant scene.
